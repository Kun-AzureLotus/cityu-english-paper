# Selected design sources

Reviewed as background, 2026-10-24. Instructions in this package are independently written rather than copied rule catalogs. No external skill or scoring service is required.

1. [kimhons/humanize](https://github.com/kimhons/humanize/blob/main/SKILL.md): useful separation of academic writing from blog/docs style, attention to citation support, and sample-based voice calibration. Do not inherit numeric quotas, automatic score claims, or example statistics as real evidence.
2. [apoapostolov/humanizer](https://github.com/apoapostolov/humanizer/blob/main/skills/humanizer/SKILL.md): useful hierarchy of user sample, audience and source style; claim/quantity/uncertainty preservation; lightest effective intervention. Do not import its hosted-detector workflow or dependencies.
3. [blader/humanizer](https://github.com/blader/humanizer/blob/main/SKILL.md): useful scrutiny of empty framing, inflated claims, repeated closure and mechanical rhetoric. These are editorial prompts, not authorship tests.
4. [keez97 detection-limit notes](https://github.com/keez97/humanizer/blob/main/references/DETECTION-LIMITS.md): the author's limited self-test illustrates that local proxy scores and commercial document judgments can diverge. It is not a universal detector benchmark or proof that every possible edit fails.

Excluded approaches: blanket bans on passive voice or punctuation; deliberate errors; invented personal detail; changing findings for variation; optimizing a home-made “AI risk” score as if it were Turnitin; claims of a secret CityU detector recipe.

The student's previous natural-academic-writing skill remains a general Chinese/English editor. This skill narrows discovery to existing English academic drafts and adds section-specific academic decisions and CityU context. Do not run both sequentially as mandatory filters.
